Arthritis Self-Management (General Educational Information)

Movement: Gentle, low-impact exercise such as walking, swimming, or
stretching helps keep joints flexible and can reduce stiffness. Long
periods of complete inactivity often make stiffness worse, but activity
should stay within a comfortable range and increase gradually.

Joint care: Alternating between activity and rest, using supportive
footwear, and avoiding repetitive strain on a single joint can reduce
flare-ups. Applying heat can ease stiffness, while cold packs can help
with swelling after activity.

Diet and weight: Maintaining a healthy weight reduces stress on
weight-bearing joints like the knees and hips. Anti-inflammatory foods
such as fatty fish, vegetables, and nuts are commonly recommended
alongside a generally balanced diet.

Warning signs that need medical attention: a joint that becomes suddenly
hot, very swollen, or intensely painful, joint pain following an injury,
fever combined with joint pain, or a rapid loss of ability to move a joint.

This information is general and educational only. It is not a diagnosis or
a treatment plan. Always confirm any changes to activity, diet, or
medication with a qualified healthcare provider.
