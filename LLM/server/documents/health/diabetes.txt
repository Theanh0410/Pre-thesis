Diabetes Self-Management (General Educational Information)

Diet: Favor whole grains, vegetables, and lean protein; limit refined sugar,
sugary drinks, and heavily processed carbohydrates. Eating at consistent
times each day helps keep blood sugar levels more stable.

Exercise: Regular light activity, such as a 20-30 minute walk most days,
helps the body use insulin more effectively. Check with a doctor before
starting a new exercise routine, especially if there are existing
complications.

Monitoring: Blood sugar should be checked as often as a doctor recommends.
Keeping a simple log of readings, meals, and activity can help a doctor
adjust treatment.

Warning signs that need prompt medical attention: confusion, excessive
thirst combined with very frequent urination, unexplained weight loss,
blurred vision that develops quickly, or a wound that is not healing.
Severe shakiness, sweating, or fainting can indicate dangerously low blood
sugar and may require immediate care.

This information is general and educational only. It is not a diagnosis or
a treatment plan. Always confirm any changes to diet, exercise, or
medication with a qualified healthcare provider.
