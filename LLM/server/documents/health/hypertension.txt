Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) Self-Management (General Educational
Information)

Diet: Reducing salt intake is one of the most effective everyday changes.
Favor fresh fruits, vegetables, and whole grains; limit processed and
canned foods, which are often high in sodium. Limiting alcohol also helps.

Exercise: Regular moderate activity, such as brisk walking, cycling, or
swimming for about 30 minutes most days, can help lower blood pressure
over time. Build up gradually and check with a doctor before starting a
new routine.

Monitoring: Home blood pressure monitors can help track trends between
doctor visits. A general guideline used by many clinicians is that
readings consistently at or above 140/90 mmHg should be discussed with a
doctor, but individual targets vary by person and should be set by a
healthcare provider.

Warning signs that need urgent medical attention: a sudden severe headache,
chest pain, shortness of breath, vision changes, confusion, or a blood
pressure reading far above a person's usual range (for example, above
180/120 mmHg) - these may indicate a hypertensive emergency.

This information is general and educational only. It is not a diagnosis or
a treatment plan. Always confirm any changes to diet, exercise, or
medication with a qualified healthcare provider.
