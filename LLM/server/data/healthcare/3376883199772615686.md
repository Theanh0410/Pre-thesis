Product Research Methodology: Our Process
At SleepFoundation.org, we conduct extensive first-person experience tests on all but a select few products. This hands-on approach allows us to provide accurate, data-driven recommendations for mattresses, pillows, sheets, and other sleep essentials.

Guidelines for our testing methodology are as follows:
Every member of our team brings years of industry experience and knowledge to our hands-on evaluations. In addition to testing products, we’ve spoken to sleep experts and toured manufacturing sites.
Our testers fall into different categories based on body type, sleep position, and personal preferences. This level of diversity helps to ensure our product ratings are holistic and inclusive of all sleepers.
Each type of sleep product is tested using a unique set of performance criteria. We also assign ratings based on feedback from testers who fall into specific categories based on weight and sleep position (i.e. side sleepers who weigh 130 to 230 pounds).
We do not personally test CPAP equipment, anti-snoring mouthguards, supplements, and other medical devices that require a doctor's prescription. Rather than using specific rating criteria, we rely on our team's extensive knowledge of sleep products and medical industry standards to pair each reader with the right devices. These evaluations are also based on material and design quality, features, and other noteworthy product attributes.

Key Takeaways
A mattress pad is a thin layer of fabric that goes between your mattress and fitted sheet, protecting your mattress from dust, allergens, sweat, and stains.
Mattress pads can provide some softness and cushioning, but they’re not as thick as a mattress topper.

How We Test: Rigor, Data, and Sleeper Feedback
Our team has years of experience evaluating bedding, and we select our top product picks after in-house testing. When examining mattress pads, we carefully assess how they feel, how much protection they provide for a mattress, and how well they regulate temperature. Our team includes a variety of different sleepers, so we can get a clear picture of how the bedding will perform for a broad range of people.
Construction analysis:
We begin our tests by taking a close look at mattress pad construction with attention to materials and special features. Analyzing construction helps us gauge performance factors such as temperature control, durability, and overall quality.
Product testing:
For the hands-on portion of our product testing, we take turns lying on a mattress with the mattress pad in place. This allows us to evaluate how securely the mattress pad stays in place, whether the bedding sleeps cool or retains heat, and how the pad feels. We also do a bit of stress testing to see how well the pad protects against accidents.
Field testing:
Our field testers evaluate different mattress pads and other sleep products in their own bedrooms. They test each pad for several weeks, making daily notes on how the bedding performs in the long term.
User testing:
We consult customer surveys and third-party reviews to get an idea of how popular each mattress pad is with shoppers and owners. This feedback lets us know if our ratings match the opinions of everyday people.
What Is a Mattress Pad?
A mattress pad is a thin layer of fabric that goes between your mattress and fitted sheet. Many mattress pads wrap around the bed on all corners with elastic.
They serve
two main purposes:
Added comfort:
Most mattress pads add a bit of softness, and some are extra cushy. They can also help you sleep more comfortably by regulating temperature (helping you sleep cooler or warmer).
Mattress protection:
Mattress pads act as a barrier against dust, allergens, sweat, and stains. Some mattress pads even have a waterproof layer to help protect your bed from spills and accidents. They won’t keep everything out, but they’ll protect your bed from a lot of issues.
Mattress Pads vs. Mattress Toppers vs. Mattress Protectors
In contrast, a
mattress topper
is thicker and designed to adjust the feel of the mattress. Mattress pads give you a little cushion, but mattress toppers provide inches of foam, latex, or down to lay on.
A
mattress encasement
(or
mattress protector
) wraps all the way around your mattress and is sealed with a zipper. Mattress protectors are the heavy duty cousin of a mattress pad, and they’re almost always waterproof.
How to Choose the Best Mattress Pad for Your Needs
When shopping for a mattress pad, it’s best to focus on your specific needs. If you’re worried about spills or accidents, you’ll want a mattress pad with waterproofing. If you sleep hot at night, you’ll want to look for breathability and cooling materials. The right mattress pad can make a serious difference in your sleep quality and the lifespan of your mattress.
Different mattress pads are designed to solve different problems, and that can make it hard to do easy apples-to-apples comparisons. We’ve provided a full breakdown of key factors you should consider while shopping. Read through to find the ones that apply to you, so you know what your perfect mattress pad looks like.
Price
Most shoppers are going to care about price, but it’s hard to know what to expect to pay for a mattress pad. The most basic, budget-friendly mattress pads can cost around $20, while high-end models can cost you $300 or more. If you’re looking for a mattress pad with special features or materials, you can expect to pay more for it.
Setting your price range before you start shopping can help you narrow down your options and keep you from wasting time looking at options outside of your budget.
Waterproofing
If you’re worried about liquid spills, bedwetting, or pet accidents, then a waterproof mattress pad is right up your alley. If you want maximum protection, then a
waterproof mattress encasement
is your best option. If you rarely have food or drinks near your bed and just want a bit of protection, a regular mattress pad is probably enough for you.
To make sure a mattress pad is actually waterproof, look for details about the layer that creates the barrier against liquids. These waterproof layers are usually made with vinyl or polyurethane.
Quality Materials
The materials used in a mattress pad directly affect its durability, comfort, and performance. Generally speaking, if you’re looking for a nicer product or a special feature, you’re going to end up paying for it.
Durable and dependable mattress pads are made of high-quality materials and tend to have better reviews. You should also consider whether you want natural, organic, or eco-friendly materials such as organic cotton over synthetics like polyester.
Cooling Properties
If you tend to sleep hot or you live in a warm climate, you’ll want to go for a
cooling mattress pad
. Most cooling mattress pads are made with breathable fabrics that pull moisture away from your body so it can evaporate. Some pads are made with specialized phase change materials (PCM) that are designed to help regulate temperature. There are also a few active cooling pads that use tubes of water to directly control the temperature of your bed.
Remember that sleeping temperature is about more than just your mattress pad – your
mattress
,
sheets
, and blankets all play a role. If you need to sleep cooler at night, you’ll want to make sure every part of your bed is set up for cooling.

Thickness, Firmness, and Pressure Relief
Thicker mattress pads like pillow-top styles add more cushion to your bed, changing the feel of your mattress. A thicker pad can add some cushioning to your bed and make it feel softer. This added cushioning can help reduce pressure on areas like your shoulders, hips, and lower back.
If your bed is already comfortably soft, or you don’t want to change how your bed feels, then avoid thick mattress pads. Also if you’re just looking for protection or waterproofing, a thinner pad is a better fit.
Fit and Pocket Depth
Many mattress pads are built with plenty of stretch, so most people don’t have to worry about a mattress pad fitting on their bed as long as they buy the right size. Double-check reviews just to make sure people aren’t complaining about the mattress pad running small, but otherwise don’t worry too much about it.
If you have a thicker than average mattress (13+ inches thick), be sure to check the dimensions and pocket depth of your mattress pad before you buy to make sure it will fit comfortably.
Ease of Cleaning
Most people want a mattress pad that’s easy to clean and maintain, especially if you’re worried about spills, stains, children, or pets. The majority of mattress pads are machine-washable and machine-driable, but there are some exceptions. Check the manufacturer’s instructions ahead of time to make sure a pad is easy to care for.
Noise
Some mattress pads can be crinkly and noisy when you lay on them, especially ones with plastic-like waterproof layers. If you’re sensitive to sounds at night, you’ll want to look for a smoother, thinner pad made of a quiet material like cotton.
Which Type of Mattress Pad Should I Choose?
Mattress pads come in a variety of materials, each with their own unique pros and cons. We’ve put together a quick breakdown of what you should know about the positives and negatives of each type of mattress pad.
Mattress Pad Type
What You Should Know
Cotton
Cotton is one of the most popular choices for mattress pads. Its performance is impressive, providing softness and moisture-wicking with virtually no noise. Most cotton is durable and can be safely machine-washed and dried.
Some types of cotton mattress pads are available at a low cost, but higher-end and organic cotton mattress pads are more expensive.
Polyester
Polyester is another of the most common materials used in mattress pads. Polyester can be thin and soft, like in athletic clothing, or brushed microfiber, which has a plush feel. Polyester can wick moisture, but it’s not as breathable as other materials.
Polyester mattress pads are often the least expensive and easy to clean, but they don’t last as long as other types of mattress pads.
Down
Down feathers are found in many bedding products, including mattress pads. Down’s light and soft feel makes it especially popular in thicker mattress pads that provide a plush sleeping surface.
The negatives to down are its high price point, its potential for causing allergies in some people, and it flattens if it gets wet.
Rayon
Rayon is a fabric derived from natural fibers, including different types of wood pulp. Rayon is pretty similar to cotton in terms of its smoothness and breathability, but it may not be quite as soft to the touch.
Rayon mattress pads are less common and tend to be pricier than polyester, but they still come in a good range of prices.
Wool
Wool is found in some mattress pads and is well-known for its breathability and natural temperature regulation that allows it to be comfortable in both winter and summer.
Wool is soft and can feel plush, but it’s usually expensive. Wool is also pretty rare in mattress pads, so there aren’t as many options available.
Waterproof
With virtually any of these materials, a waterproofing layer can be added to keep liquids from seeping into a mattress. The most common form of waterproofing is a lining or backing made of vinyl or thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU).
What Are the Benefits of a Mattress Pad?
Mattress pads meet a variety of needs, so it’s not surprising that using one comes with a variety of benefits. Here’s a quick breakdown of the top benefits of using a mattress pad:
Mattress protection:
A mattress pad acts as a barrier, helping to protect your bed from stains, spills, dust, and accidents. Waterproof pads go the extra mile to protect your bed against liquids, but even a regular mattress pad can stop some mess from reaching your mattress. Mattress pads can help your mattress last longer, especially if you’re worried about accidents.
Allergen defence:
A mattress pad can help protect your mattress from allergens and dust mites. If you want the strongest allergen prevention you can get, use a mattress encasement instead.
Added comfort:
The feel of a mattress pad can make your mattress softer and more comfortable, especially if you use a thicker model made with down or wool. Cooling mattress pads can help hot sleepers rest easier without overheating.
Easy to clean:
Trying to spot-clean a mattress without damaging it can be a pain, which is why people use mattress pads instead. Most mattress pads are easy to wash and dry, so accidents are much more manageable when you use a mattress pad.
Cost-effective:
Compared to mattress toppers and mattress encasements, mattress pads are much more budget-friendly. Mattress pads won’t completely change the feel of your bed or provide extreme mattress protection, but they’re a good, well-rounded option that meets the needs of most people.

Frequently Asked Questions About Mattress Pads
If you’re still wondering about whether a mattress pad is right for your bed, we’ve put together answers to some frequently asked questions to address your remaining doubts.
How Does a Mattress Pad Attach to the Bed?
Almost all mattress pads use elastic to connect to the bed and stay in place.
The most common way that this elastic is deployed is by sewing it into the perimeter of the pad. A skirt or side panel goes over the sides of the mattress, and the skirt has elastic placed either in the corners only or in a continuous band that goes around the full hem. In this way, these mattress pads resemble a fitted sheet.
In some cases, a mattress pad does not have a skirt or side panels and uses elastic straps in each corner to go under the mattress and hold the pad in place. Pads with straps are prone to shifting around and often need to be reattached.
Can Mattress Pads Adjust the Firmness of My Bed?
Most mattress pads provide a touch of softness to a bed, but don’t change the feel too much. Some thicker pads made with materials like down feathers can make a bed feel even softer.
If your goal is to dramatically change the feel of your bed, then you’re better off buying a mattress topper instead of a mattress pad.
How Do I Clean My Mattress Pad?
Follow the manufacturer’s instructions to clean any specific mattress pad – they know their product best.
Generally speaking, most mattress pads can be machine-washed and machine-dried on gentle or low-heat settings, but the best washer and dryer settings can vary depending on the pad’s materials. Some pads also can’t be cleaned in a washer or dryer, so double-check the manufacturer’s instructions.
What’s the Difference Between a Mattress Pad and a Mattress Topper or Mattress Protector?
A mattress pad is thinner and designed to provide a bit of comfort and protection for your mattress. Mattress toppers are thicker and meant to change the feel of a bed when you lay on it. Mattress protectors (or mattress encasements) surround a mattress on all sides and are meant for heavy duty protection.
Each of these products serves a different core need. If your focus is maximum protection against spills and allergens, you’ll want a mattress encasement. If your focus is on maximum change to the feel of the mattress, a mattress topper is best. If you want something that falls in between, defending the mattress while lightly changing its feel, then a mattress pad is a good middle ground.
