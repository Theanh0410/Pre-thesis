Product Research Methodology: Our Process
At SleepFoundation.org, we conduct extensive first-person experience tests on all but a select few products. This hands-on approach allows us to provide accurate, data-driven recommendations for mattresses, pillows, sheets, and other sleep essentials.

Guidelines for our testing methodology are as follows:
Every member of our team brings years of industry experience and knowledge to our hands-on evaluations. In addition to testing products, we’ve spoken to sleep experts and toured manufacturing sites.
Our testers fall into different categories based on body type, sleep position, and personal preferences. This level of diversity helps to ensure our product ratings are holistic and inclusive of all sleepers.
Each type of sleep product is tested using a unique set of performance criteria. We also assign ratings based on feedback from testers who fall into specific categories based on weight and sleep position (i.e. side sleepers who weigh 130 to 230 pounds).
We do not personally test CPAP equipment, anti-snoring mouthguards, supplements, and other medical devices that require a doctor's prescription. Rather than using specific rating criteria, we rely on our team's extensive knowledge of sleep products and medical industry standards to pair each reader with the right devices. These evaluations are also based on material and design quality, features, and other noteworthy product attributes.

You should consider a cooling comforter if you often overheat and kick off blankets during the night. Cooling comforters typically feature more breathable materials and a more lightweight design than traditional
quilts, comforters, and duvets
.

If you get cold easily at night, a cooling comforter may not provide enough insulation to keep you warm. You should also avoid a cooling comforter if you enjoy the heft of a thicker and heavier blanket.

What Is the Best Type of Comforter for Cooling?
Comforters are often categorized by the type of fill, which varies in breathability and insulation. Fill material influences a comforter’s overall performance, including its cooling properties. The material of the shell also affects a comforter’s temperature-regulating capability.
The materials that are most often found in cooling comforters include:
Wool:
Wool is most commonly made from the fleece of sheep. It is well-regarded for its strong temperature regulation and it can wick moisture away from your body if you sweat. The fibers are also breathable, which helps prevent too much heat from building up. Keep in mind that wool is a more expensive material and is usually more difficult to maintain than some other materials found in comforters.
Cotton:
Cotton is prized for its breathability and softness. Air flows easily through cotton fibers, allowing heat to escape instead of building up. However, not all cotton is created equal, and bedding made with high-end cotton carries a higher price-point.
Silk:
Derived from the cocoons of silkworms, silk is lightweight, smooth, and extremely breathable. Silk also has a luxurious sheen that can elevate your bedroom decor. Silk bedding typically costs more than other types of bedding, and it requires careful handling to avoid damage.
Down:
Down comes from the soft layer of quill-less feathers found underneath the outer plumage of ducks and geese. While thicker down comforters tend to retain heat, you can also find lightweight options that are more breathable. The downsides to down include its higher price-point, higher maintenance, and potential to trigger allergies.
Down Alternative:
Down alternative has similar characteristics to down but is made from synthetic materials like polyester and microfiber. As a result,
down alternative comforters
are generally less expensive, easier to clean, and more hypoallergenic than down models. They also tend to be more breathable. That said, down alternative comforters may not have the same longevity as down comforters.
Rayon and Lyocell:
Wood pulp from bamboo or eucalyptus plants can be processed to create fabrics, such as rayon or lyocell. The latter is often referred to by the brand name Tencel. These fabrics are breathable, moisture-wicking, and soft. Be aware that bamboo- and eucalyptus-derived materials vary widely in price and quality.
What to Consider When Buying a Cooling Comforter
When shopping for a comforter, it’s easy to get overwhelmed by all the available options. We’ll cover some key factors to help you zero in on a high-performance cooling comforter.
Cooling Properties
Comforters that feature strong cooling materials and designs can help regulate your body temperature. Moisture-wicking and breathable fabrics allow heat to escape so that you don’t overheat. Lightweight construction with low-density fill can also enhance a comforter’s temperature regulation.
Size and Weight
A comforter’s weight or thickness can significantly impact its temperature regulation. Heavier comforters tend to trap more heat and can feel too warm for hot sleepers. Lighter or thinner comforters provide less insulation, allowing more heat to dissipate while you sleep.
Be sure to buy the right
comforter size
that matches the dimensions of your mattress. A comforter should drape over the sides of your bed without touching the floor.
Style and Design
Many shoppers prioritize cooling comforters that complement the decor of their bedroom. Some design factors to consider are the color and stitching pattern on the comforter’s shell. Certain materials and weaves have a sleek and shiny look, while others have a plain or matte finish.
Most comforters are available in limited color options. If you want more choices for colors or patterns, you may want to consider adding a duvet cover.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do cooling comforters actually work?
If they are made with breathable and lightweight materials, cooling comforters can help prevent heat buildup, wick away moisture, and keep you at a pleasant temperature throughout the night.
How much does a cooling comforter cost?
The average price range for cooling comforters is approximately $50 to $500. Higher-quality options generally start at $100. Pricing is determined by the type and quality of materials as well as the brand and the size of the comforter.
How do you clean a cooling comforter?
A cooling comforter should always be cleaned according to the manufacturer’s instructions, which are designed to prevent damage to the materials. Many comforters can be machine-washed with cold water and mild detergent and then dried on a low heat or tumble dry setting. However, some comforters require more delicate handling, such as spot-cleaning or line drying.
How long does a cooling comforter last?
A cooling comforter made with high-quality materials may last up to 15 years or more if properly cared for. Comforters made with less durable materials may only last for a few years. For any comforter, heavy use, improper care, and damage from pets can reduce its lifespan.
