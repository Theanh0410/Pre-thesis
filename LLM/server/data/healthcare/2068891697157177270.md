Product Research Methodology: Our Process
At SleepFoundation.org, we conduct extensive first-person experience tests on all but a select few products. This hands-on approach allows us to provide accurate, data-driven recommendations for mattresses, pillows, sheets, and other sleep essentials.

Guidelines for our testing methodology are as follows:
Every member of our team brings years of industry experience and knowledge to our hands-on evaluations. In addition to testing products, we’ve spoken to sleep experts and toured manufacturing sites.
Our testers fall into different categories based on body type, sleep position, and personal preferences. This level of diversity helps to ensure our product ratings are holistic and inclusive of all sleepers.
Each type of sleep product is tested using a unique set of performance criteria. We also assign ratings based on feedback from testers who fall into specific categories based on weight and sleep position (i.e. side sleepers who weigh 130 to 230 pounds).
We do not personally test CPAP equipment, anti-snoring mouthguards, supplements, and other medical devices that require a doctor's prescription. Rather than using specific rating criteria, we rely on our team's extensive knowledge of sleep products and medical industry standards to pair each reader with the right devices. These evaluations are also based on material and design quality, features, and other noteworthy product attributes.

Up-to-Date
From smart alarms and tracking devices to eye masks and comfy pajamas, sleep products can help you get the rest you need so you can wake up feeling refreshed. Everyone has different needs and preferences, so you should base which sleep products you use on your own personal criteria.
People tend to focus on mattresses, pillows, and bedding when it comes to optimizing their sleep space. These are all essential to a good night’s rest, but you can invest in additional products to help you relax before bed, curtail sleep disruptions during the night, and wake up feeling refreshed each morning.
Pajamas, eye masks, and other wearable accessories can help you feel more comfortable in bed prior to and during sleep. You can also monitor your sleep patterns with a tracking device, and improve your sleep quality and duration with gadgets like smart alarms, wake-up lights, and white noise machines. Lastly, products like blackout curtains and sleep sprays can help promote a restful bedroom environment.

What Are Sleep Products?
For the purposes of this guide, sleep products is a catchall category referring to any accessory that promotes restfulness and healthy sleep. While all sleep products serve this general purpose, they may achieve it through different means.
Some products enhance your sleep space with inviting scents, soothing music, or by reducing light and noise interference. Others track sleep data using sensors and smart apps. The term may also apply to sleepwear, alarm clocks, humidifiers, and other items that make up your dedicated sleep space.
Pajamas and Accessories
While many people
sleep in the nude
, a large number of adults prefer wearing pajamas to bed. Traditional pajamas consist of a top and bottoms, but a wide range of styles and combinations are available. Common fabrics for pajamas made today include cotton, wool, Tencel, and rayon derived from bamboo. Other wearable accessories for sleep may include an eye mask and earplugs, which curb sleep disruptions by blocking light and noise, respectively.
Gadgets and Tech
Many people employ tracking devices with built-in sensors to monitor their sleep patterns and provide daily data. You can use this data to improve your sleep routine, and many trackers include companion apps with sleep tutorials and coaching programs.
Sleep trackers
may be wearable, or consist of a thin sheet that rests on top of your mattress.
Other gadgets that can help you sleep better include sunrise alarms that mimic natural light patterns to help you wake up comfortably, noise-canceling headphones to muffle loud noises at night, and white noise machines.
Sleeping Environment
Optimizing the area where you sleep is key to a good night’s rest. Aromatherapy and sleep sprays both use essential oils to create relaxing scents that promote calm and relaxation. If you live on a brightly lit street or typically sleep during the day, blackout curtains can prevent natural or artificial light from entering your bedroom. The right bedroom fan can help maintain a comfortable temperature for sleep.
Sleep aids
like melatonin also fall into this category, but you should always speak to your doctor before taking any sort of supplement.
What to Consider When Buying a Sleep Product
When choosing a new sleep product, your personal needs and preferences should come first and foremost. A crucial first step is determining why you want to invest in a particular product. For example, people who are struggling to relax before bed and fall asleep should use a product that serves this specific purpose, as opposed to a product that helps them stay asleep or wake up feeling refreshed.
The best product for another sleeper may not be right for you, and could potentially prevent you from getting the rest you need. Many sleep products come with trial periods allowing you to test them in your bedroom and decide whether they’re helpful or not.
Frequently Asked Questions
How much sleep do I need?
According to current recommendations, adults 18 and older should get
at least seven hours of sleep each night
. Occasionally falling below this threshold shouldn’t impact your health too much, but not getting enough sleep on a regular basis can negatively impact your physical, mental, and emotional well-being.
Practicing
healthy sleep hygiene
can help you get the sleep you need. Chronic lack of sleep may indicate you have a sleep disorder such as
insomnia
. If you aren’t getting enough sleep and don’t know why, talk to your doctor.
What products are best for hot sleepers?
If you regularly sleep hot or sweat in your sleep, you should consider a sleep product that promotes cooling and temperature control. Your options include pajamas and other sleep garments made from moisture-wicking fabrics, an adjustable bedside fan, a humidifier for your bedroom, or a mattress pad with active cooling technology.
It’s important to maintain a bedroom temperature that’s comfortable for sleep. Studies show a thermostat setting of
60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit
is ideal for most adult sleepers.
What products are best for heavy sleepers?
Heavy sleepers typically need alarms and other devices that are loud or strong enough to wake them from their deep slumber. Some alarms are designed to become progressively louder, so they can wake you up without harsh initial blaring. Another option is a sunrise alarm, which uses gradually brightening colors to sync with your circadian rhythm and help you wake up without any noise. Some mattress pads and sleep trackers also include alarms that wake you up with gentle vibrations.
How do light and noise affect sleep?
Light can interfere with the natural circadian rhythms guiding your sleep cycle. Our bodies are trained to feel alert in the presence of daylight and more relaxed after the sun goes down. If your
body detects light
during periods of natural nighttime darkness, this can cause you to wake up. Loud noises at night can also affect your sleep cycle by reducing the time you spend in deep sleep, which in turn may cause you to feel tired or groggy in the morning.
Some products specifically target these sleep deterrents. Options include eye masks and blackout curtains to reduce light interference, and earplugs, sleep headphones, and white noise machines to drown out loud environmental sounds.
What if these products are not helping me?
Finding the most helpful sleep products may require some trial and error on your part. Thankfully, many sleep products come with trial periods. You can test the product in your sleep space, experiment with settings, and decide whether it’s worth the investment.
Be sure to carefully review your trial options for each product. While some brands allow you to use their products during the trial, others do not accept returns for products that have been removed from their packaging.
