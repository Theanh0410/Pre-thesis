Waking up with a sore jaw or headache can be a sign that you’re grinding your teeth at night — a condition known as
bruxism
. Currently, there is no
cure for bruxism
. Left untreated, teeth grinding can cause extensive damage to your teeth, interfere with your sleep, and frustrate your bed partner. Thankfully, using a mouthguard specifically designed for teeth grinding can protect your teeth and help you get the rest you need.
In this guide, we’ll introduce our team’s picks for the best mouthguards for teeth grinding. We go over what features to consider when looking for a mouthguard, share some of the best brands on the market, and address common questions to help you make an informed decision.
Keep in mind that mouthguards can offer significant relief for many, but they don’t work for everyone. We recommend consulting your dentist or healthcare provider to understand the underlying causes of your teeth grinding and discuss the most effective treatment options.

What to Look for in a Mouthguard for Teeth Grinding
Mouthguards help to prevent teeth grinding by creating a barrier between the teeth. These simple devices can
protect teeth from wear and tear
caused by clenching and grinding, as well as reduce tension in the jaw muscles.
We’ve created the following guide to walk you through the different types of mouthguards for teeth grinding and help you consider materials, fit, and pricing to find the best one for your teeth.
Mouthguard Types
There are several types of mouthguards to consider, each with its own benefits and potential drawbacks. While the best choice is likely a mouthguard custom-fitted by a dentist, understanding over-the-counter options can help you find the type that will work with your needs and budget.
Mouthguard Type
Description
One-size-fits-all
Ready to wear straight from the packaging, these mouthguards are the most cost-effective option. Although they are convenient and inexpensive, their generic fit won’t work for everyone, potentially leading to discomfort and
less effective prevention of teeth grinding
.
Boil-and-bite
These mouthguards are typically made from thermoplastics, meaning they can be heated in water and placed in your mouth to form a custom fit. They offer a good balance between customizability and cost, but
don’t offer as precise a fit as custom options
.
Mail-in custom
Custom mouthguards offer the highest level of personalization. They involve creating a dental impression at home, which you then mail to a lab to have the mouthguard crafted to fit your teeth. While this option is more expensive than the other two types, it provides protection from teeth grinding second only to a mouthguard fitted by a dentist.
It’s important to note that other types of mouthguards, like those for sports or snoring, are not effective at preventing teeth grinding. These are designed for different purposes and are not a substitute for seeing a dentist or finding a mouthguard made specifically for teeth grinding.
Fit and Comfort
A mouthguard needs to be well-fitted to ensure comfort and effectiveness. Custom-fit options can be made from dental impressions that can be taken at home or your dentist’s office. Custom mouthguards ensure the best fit by conforming to the unique contours of your teeth and gums.
Some brands give you the choice between mouthguards for your upper or lower teeth. The choice between an upper or lower mouthguard often comes down to personal preferences and what’s effective in eliminating nighttime teeth grinding. When in doubt, talk to your dentist for support in finding the right mouthguard or other treatment approach.
Materials and Durability
Mouthguards are typically made from different types of plastics, like vinyl or acrylic. The choice of material can affect a mouthguard’s comfort and durability. High-quality materials can better withstand nightly grinding without wearing out quickly. Opting for a durable mouthguard not only offers better protection but also proves more cost-effective in the long run.
Many people who grind and clench their teeth find that dual-layer mouthguards, which combine a tough outer layer with a softer inner layer, are comfortable without sacrificing durability. Discussing options with your dentist is the best way to determine the most effective choice for you.
Pricing and Where to Buy
The cost of over-the-counter mouthguards varies widely, typically ranging from $20 for simple over-the-counter options to $200 or more for custom-fit models. Factors influencing the price include the type of mouthguard, the brand, whether it’s custom-made, and policies the company holds, like free shipping or free adjustments. You can purchase mouthguards at retail stores, online, or get them custom fitted from your dentist.
Ease of Cleaning
Whether you choose a one-size-fits-all mouthguard or a custom-fitted design, most are comparable in terms of how easily they are to clean. Clean your mouthguard based on the manufacturer’s instructions. Most often, this entails rinsing it under warm water and brushing it with a toothbrush. Allow it to air-dry completely before storing it in a ventilated container to prevent bacterial growth. Some brands ask that you store your mouthguard in a glass of water.
What Causes Teeth Grinding at Night?
Teeth grinding, or bruxism, is when a person clenches their jaw and grinds their teeth against one another. Teeth grinding can occur among adults or
children
. While it’s more common to grind your teeth during the day, nighttime teeth grinding can be especially problematic because it’s
more challenging to stop
.
Although dentists can’t always pinpoint the exact cause, grinding can be triggered by stress, medications, smoking, caffeine, and even a person’s genetics. Researchers have found that teeth grinding is more common in people with certain sleep problems, including
obstructive sleep apnea
and parasomnias like sleep talking and sleepwalking.
Teeth grinding can damage the teeth and increase sensitivity to hot, cold, and sugary foods. Without treatment, grinding can also lead to problems with the jaw. Other symptoms of teeth grinding include:
Anxiety
or depression
Ear pain
Headaches
Eating problems
Symptoms of teeth grinding can make it harder to sleep and the noise from grinding can affect bed partners.
While this guide provides information to help you understand teeth grinding and find the best  mouthguards, we’re not offering medical advice or treatment. If you suspect that you may be grinding your teeth at night, it’s important to discuss these concerns with a doctor or dentist.
