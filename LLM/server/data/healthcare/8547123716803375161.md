Product Research Methodology: Our Process
Here at SleepFoundation.org, we test all mattress toppers featured on our review pages in-house. Our extensive testing process ensures our product recommendations for toppers are as accurate as possible.
Our testing methodology guidelines include the following:
Our testers vary widely in their sleep preferences and body types and have multiple years of experience testing mattress toppers. This diversity provides us with comprehensive and inclusive data on which we base our recommendations.
All mattress toppers we test in Sleep Doctor’s Seattle office undergo rounds of testing for several performance criteria. We test each topper on a medium soft (4) mattress, a medium (5) mattress, a medium firm (6) mattress, and a firm (7) mattress to ensure well-rounded ratings.
Testers lie down on the mattress topper and move across the surface while we use heat guns, pressure mapping tools, and VOC meters to test for temperature control, pressure relief, and off-gassing, respectively. We then use this data to give the topper ratings for each of our performance categories.
While lying on the mattress topper, our testers rate comfort and support in their preferred position. Based on their feedback, we assign ratings for the topper’s performance across sleeping positions and body weights.

Up-to-Date
A mattress topper is a layer of cushioning material that can be placed on top of a mattress to make the surface feel softer. Most toppers measure 1 to 3 inches thick, so using one on a mattress is essentially the same as adding an extra comfort layer. You should consider a topper if you find your current mattress too firm, or if the bed’s comfort layers have begun to deteriorate due to age. A topper won’t “fix” an old mattress that needs to be replaced, but it can provide a
temporary fix
if your bed no longer feels comfortable.
Memory foam
is one of the most popular materials for toppers. Also known as viscoelastic polyurethane foam, memory foam softens when exposed to body heat and creates a deep, pressure-relieving cradle as you slowly sink into the material. The close contouring of memory foam can alleviate pressure for any sleeper, and also improve spinal alignment for side sleepers who don’t receive enough padding for their shoulders and hips.
Our best memory foam mattress topper guide will take a closer look at these products in terms of pricing, construction, and performance in different areas. Below, we’ve listed our top picks among memory foam toppers sold today. Each selection is based on a combination of verified owner experiences and in-house evaluations from our internal testing and research.

At first glance, most memory foam toppers will appear to be quite similar. However, there are several factors to take into account when comparing different

What to Consider When Purchasing a Memory Foam Mattress Topper
When browsing different memory foam toppers, you should be wary of certain claims from manufacturers. For instance, “universal comfort” is a common exaggeration. Each topper is constructed for a specific firmness level that will make it more comfortable for certain people based on their body type and sleep position, and less so for others.
Rather than focusing on marketing jargon, you should use the following factors to guide your search for the best memory foam topper.
Type of Foam
Check for density when comparing memory foam toppers. Denser foams offer better durability, more consistent motion isolation, and deeper contouring without too much sink. Less dense foams, on the other hand, are usually more breathable and won’t emit as much off-gassing odor. If the topper is composed of certain types of memory foam, then it may also sleep noticeably cooler. Some of the best cooling memory foam mattress topper models are made from open-cell memory foam, which promotes steady airflow and feels more breathable as a result.
Price
The average memory foam mattress topper costs between $150 and $300 in a queen size. Keep in mind that a high sticker price does not necessarily mean the topper is superior to its more affordable counterparts. In fact, some high-quality memory foam toppers cost less than $150 in a queen. Customer reviews on topper product pages can be helpful resources for determining whether a topper has a fair price or is too expensive.
Sleeping Position
Side sleepers generally prefer softer toppers that cushion the shoulders and hips, align the spine, and alleviate pressure throughout the body. Comparatively, most back and stomach sleepers need a firmer topper that will keep their bodies on an even plane without sinking too much. Most toppers are designed to be softer in order to adjust the feel of your mattress, so be sure to inquire about firmness levels before picking a model.
Quality Materials
The quality of memory foam used in a topper can affect different performance factors, including durability, pressure relief, and temperature neutrality. Denser memory foam won’t deteriorate as quickly and delivers the most even conforming, whereas open-cell memory foam is more breathable for hot sleepers. The best
gel memory foam
mattress topper models also sleep cooler than average, though gel may not affect the topper’s breathability to a noticeable extent.
Firmness Level
The majority of memory foam toppers are considered soft, medium soft, or medium. This is because most toppers are meant to make a firmer mattress feel softer. If you sleep on your side or back and/or weigh more than 230 pounds, you may want to consider a firmer topper instead. This ensures minimal sinkage and less added pressure.
Thickness
Most toppers measure at least 1 inch thick, but those measuring 2 to 3 inches thick are more effective at adjusting the feel of your mattress. However, keep in mind that thicker toppers can also make your mattress taller, which can cause difficulty for some when they get in and out of bed.
Pressure Relief
The best memory foam mattress topper for
back pain
and pressure will conform evenly to your body without sinking too much between the shoulders and hips, where many people carry the majority of their weight. For this reason, a firmer topper will provide better pressure relief for people who weigh more than 230 pounds. Softer toppers will contour more closely to people who weigh less than 130 pounds, and this can also reduce pressure points.
Frequently Asked Questions About Memory Foam Mattress Toppers
What size memory foam mattress topper is best for me?
We generally recommend choosing a mattress topper size based on the size of your current mattress. Many toppers are equipped with corner straps or ties that help to stabilize the layer and prevent it from shifting during the night. That said, you may want to consider a topper with a smaller size if you and your sleep partner have different firmness preferences; this allows both of you to feel equally comfortable.
How do I clean a memory foam mattress topper?
Memory foam can be permanently damaged in a washer and dryer, so you should never attempt to launder these items in household machines. Spot clean with water and mild detergent if spills or stains occur on the foam. Thankfully, many memory foam toppers come with removable covers that are machine washable. In some cases, the covers are also made from antimicrobial materials that help prevent the spread of dust mites, mold, and other allergens that can make the topper less hygienic. If the cover is made of cotton or other natural fibers, then it may shrink a bit after the first laundry cycle; you may want to tumble dry on low heat or air dry to prevent excessive sinkage.
How do I store memory foam mattress toppers?
When not in use, we recommend storing your memory foam topper in a cool, dry place where it won’t be exposed to extreme temperatures or humidity. This helps to cut down on mold and mildew, as well as dust mite infestation. For added protection, you can invest in a zippered cover to fit around the topper when you aren’t using it. If you use the topper every night, then be sure to clean the cover on a regular basis.
How can you dispose of a memory foam mattress topper?
If you’d like to get rid of your topper, consider donating it to a charitable organization in your city or town. Like mattresses and other bedding accessories, toppers are in high demand and will be accepted at most donation outlets. You can also recycle the topper rather than tossing it into a landfill. If your sleep trial for the topper has not expired, contact the manufacturer to see if an exchange or refund is available. You may also qualify for a free replacement topper if it has developed a defect and the warranty hasn’t expired.
Can a memory foam mattress topper cause back pain?
While a memory foam topper is intended to reduce aches and pressure points, the wrong model can actually exacerbate your discomfort if you experience frequent back pain. If the topper is too soft, then you may sink too much and feel more pain in the shoulders, lower back, or hips – especially if you weigh more than 230 pounds. Alternately, toppers that feel too firm can cause more back pain for people who weigh less than 130 pounds. If you feel back pain while using a topper, then it may be time to replace it – and possibly your mattress, as well. You may also want to choose a topper with a different firmness level if excessive softness or firmness is to blame for the new back pain.
