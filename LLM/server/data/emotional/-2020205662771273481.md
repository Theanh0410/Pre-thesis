Online therapy can be a convenient, affordable, and accessible alternative to in-person therapy. It also works: As a licensed social worker and mental health professional (and as someone who goes to therapy), I know how helpful it can be to talk issues through with a trained professional.  Research says that online therapy is just as effective as in-person therapy, especially when using tried-and-true approaches like
cognitive behavioral therapy
and treating
anxiety
,
depression
, and
PTSD
.
I’ve spent the last three years reporting on the best online therapy services. During that time, with the help of a team of researchers and testers, I’ve reviewed 55 different online therapy companies, supervised thousands of hours of testing, and pored over thousands of survey responses, all while evaluating important things like
pricing
,
insurance acceptance
, and services offered.
I am uniquely qualified to recommend online therapy services, and yet, when someone asks me what the best online therapy company is, my answer is always, “it depends.” As with most healthcare-related things, there's no one-size-fits-all answer. The best online therapy service for you will be unique to your needs, lifestyle, insurance coverage, and more.

BetterHelp

Brightside
—a master in anxiety and depression treatment, two of the biggest mental health challenges around these days.

What to Expect from Online Therapy
How much does it cost?
The services we recommend cost anywhere from $55 per session to over $250.
Who is it for?
Someone who wants to work through challenges, improve their overall wellbeing, or receive help for a mild or moderate mental health condition. It is
not
for people dealing with serious mental illness or who are in crisis or suicidal.
Will insurance cover it?
Maybe. Most companies list the insurance plans they accept or let you search for your plan when signing up. We’ve noted the online therapy services on our list who accept insurance.
How does it work?
Generally, to get started, you fill out an intake form or questionnaire. Be sure to read through the privacy policy to better understand whether it is HIPAA-compliant (HIPAA is the law that
protects personal health information
) and whether it shares any private information with third parties; we recommend opting out of data collection. Then you'll schedule an appointment to talk with your therapist over video call, phone, or text.
What’s the time commitment?
Sometimes you can start feeling better within a few weeks, and other times you might need to work with your therapist for months or even years.
Does online therapy work?
That depends on your commitment to doing the hard stuff, the skill of your therapist, and the services offered by the company. There’s no guarantee. But, if you find the right therapist and put in the effort, you’ll be surprised at how effective it is.

What I Like About Talkspace
Talkspace
is the real deal in my book: a one-stop shop for different kinds of therapy with a variety of communication options and psychiatry services as well, all covered by a slew of insurance plans. This means that basically, whatever you need—individual therapy, couples therapy, teen therapy, psychiatry and medication management—you can find it at Talkspace. And we did.
To date I’ve had ten people from all across the country try out Talkspace and overall, they were thrilled with their experiences. My testers were connected with therapists who were highly professional, warm, and accommodating. In one instance, a tester’s therapist was immediately able to suggest a solution to a problem my tester thought was unsolvable; in another, my tester’s therapist messaged her on a holiday—unprompted and outside of her regular session time—reminding her to breathe and take time for herself.
In every case, we were able to immediately relate to our therapists, making our time with them that much easier and more useful.
Another thing my testers loved about Talkspace was the communication options. The cheapest subscription at Talkspace—only $69 a week—offers “unlimited messaging” with your therapist, which went over especially well with our teen tester. (To compare, BetterHelp also offers text-based therapy sessions, but for the same price as video sessions.) Other subscriptions—from $99 to $120 per week—gave us access to weekly video sessions and workshops as well as messaging. This sense of connection with our therapists greatly enhanced the good rapport they had built, and being able to receive help and support between sessions really set Talkspace apart.
In addition to testing, I also surveyed 100 current and recent users of Talkspace to get a better understanding of the general Talkspace experience, and the company did not disappoint: 92 percent of users rated Talkspace as very good or excellent overall, which is better than 90 percent of the companies we examined.
69 percent of those surveyed reported that their Talkspace therapist met all of their needs, a percentage only narrowly exceeded by two other companies.
What Users Say
“I got the impression that [my therapist] remembered the information I told her before and did not feel like I had to repeat myself. At the end of each session, she guided me through a breathing exercise...While the goal of each exercise was the same (to center me and transition out of the session), the specifics of each exercise was slightly different, which I found engaging.”
- Talkspace tester who is a licensed therapist herself
Things to Consider
While the texting-only plan is an affordable option, it’s also my biggest hesitation about Talkspace—$69 per week that doesn’t include a single video session. As a mental health professional, I do not recommend text-only sessions: your therapist is missing out on important visual and verbal cues when they can’t see or hear you that might otherwise help them guide the session and intuit what you are feeling.
When we tried texting only, we felt the conversation was always stilted and we didn’t get as much out of it as we did when we could speak to our therapist. So it’s unfortunate that the text-based sessions are the company’s most affordable subscription. With other plans, it’s also unfortunate that the sessions are only 30 minutes long; in almost every case, we wished they were longer.

What I Like About BetterHelp
I’ve had eight testers from all across the globe try
BetterHelp
, and to me, the best thing by far about BetterHelp is its accessibility. We could access its services from anywhere in the United States and internationally (I had one tester in the UK). Even in Kansas and Missouri—places where
mental health care can be hard to find
—we were able to schedule sessions with our BetterHelp therapists that were convenient (evening and weekend scheduling wasn’t a problem) and consistent (there are no long wait lists and with plenty of therapists available, even in rural areas).
And speaking of availability, the other great thing about BetterHelp, IMHO, is the myriad of ways you can interact with your therapist. My testers loved that they could talk with their therapists via video call, phone call, or text—whichever worked best for them at the time. This flexibility makes BetterHelp’s online therapy services even more accessible, especially since other companies (like Talkspace) make you choose which communication option you’d like when you sign up.
But what about the therapy itself? Overall, our experiences can be summed up as: decent. For the most part, testers connected with their therapists and felt as though their providers were attentive and offered valuable insights and suggestions. Establishing rapport with your therapist is one of the most important aspects of therapy, and BetterHelp generally succeeds. But one of my testers described BetterHelp’s services as “therapy light;” scratching the surface without ever really diving in deep.
Still, I recommend BetterHelp for the way it is simply
there
for you, no matter who you are or where you live.
In my survey of 100 current and former BetterHelp clients, their experiences reflected those of my testers—that is to say, middle of the road. Eighty-seven percent rated BetterHelp as very good or excellent overall, which was the middle of the pack overall. And when it came to rating the qualifications of their therapists, BetterHelp was on the low side; 87% rated their therapist’s qualifications as very good or excellent, while the vast majority of the other 54 companies had ratings in the low to mid-90s.
What Users Say
“Since BetterHelp has providers in every state and many of these providers have flexible scheduling options, I think this could be a really great option for someone without access to an in-person therapist or with a full schedule.”
- BetterHelp tester from Missouri
Things to Consider
I've had several testers run into issues, from their first assigned therapist never showing up for their session to technical difficulties with their video appointments to their therapist making assumptions about them based on their visible disability. With 34,000 providers and counting, some issues are to be expected, but your time and trust are precious.
BetterHelp's prices also vary based on your location, the availability of therapists in your area, and the kind of care you need. In our testing, this meant that testers paid anywhere between $70 and $100 per week in different locations for the same exact treatment. We didn't find this kind of pay structure at any of the other companies we tested.

What I Like About Brightside
As a therapist, I recognize the benefit of specializing in certain treatments or mental health concerns—in the world of mental health, it’s better to do one thing and do it well rather than being a jack of all trades, master of none. This is why
Brightside
stands out to me: it focuses specifically on treating anxiety and depression and related disorders, and it does so extremely successfully.
I had four testers try out Brightside to tackle their anxiety and depression, and the company did not let us down. Here, the details really stood out: For example, testers were able to message their therapists between sessions at no extra cost, and not only were the responses prompt, they were also clearly well thought out, empathetic, and direct—compared to other online therapy companies, where our therapists’ responses were often delayed and even then usually just encouraged us to remember to bring up whatever issue we’d asked about in our next therapy session. Instead, Brightside therapists really made us feel supported and cared for at all times.
Brightside also did what it promised to: effectively treat anxiety and depression. In one case, my tester had just started to have panic attacks; but after only a few sessions with her Brightside therapist, she felt that her newfound coping skills made her less reliant on medication. (Another great thing about Brightside, though, is that it also offers psychiatry services. So, if you
do
need medication, you’re covered.)
My other testers appreciated the homework they were assigned, which helped them practice the
cognitive behavioral therapy
techniques that they learned in their sessions (CBT is considered the gold standard when it comes to the treatment of anxiety and depression).
And because my testers were able to choose their own therapists, they could make sure they were working with providers who really understood them, their identities, and their unique issues—something only two other companies on this list, Teladoc and Doctor on Demand, offer as well.
The 100 Brightside users I surveyed had equally positive experiences. When asked to rate their therapist’s qualifications, 94% said they were very good or excellent—one of the highest scores across all 55 companies I reviewed. In addition, a whopping 99% said they were likely or very likely to recommend Brightside to a friend or someone like them.
What Users Say
“My therapist was warm and made me feel at ease. My first impression was that she seemed kind and relatable (we are both Hispanic and have similar cultures). There was no pressure to unload everything immediately. She made sure I felt comfortable.”
- Brightside tester who was seeking treatment for anxiety and panic attacks
Things to Consider
Finding the right therapist can take some trial and error. In one instance, my tester didn’t click with her first therapist: she felt like the therapist was dismissive of the challenges she was bringing up. In another case, the person conducting my tester’s intake session was cold and unapproachable. However, both problems were easily remedied—the first tester simply switched therapists and found a great match the second time, and the second tester had a much better experience with her actual therapist with whom she connected after the intake.
Also, the process of choosing a therapist was a little strange; we had to choose a date and time for our session and were
then
shown details about the therapist who was available, rather than the other way around.

What I Like About Teladoc
My four
Teladoc
testers absolutely loved this company, and I can easily see why. I really like the fact that testers were able to choose their own therapist, rather than being assigned one—to me, this gets the client involved in the therapeutic process right from the start, and empowers them to be active in their therapy. (This differs from most online therapy companies, including BetterHelp, Talkspace, and Brightside, which use an algorithm to match clients with providers.)
Each tester’s therapist came prepared for their first session, having clearly familiarized themselves with the extensive intake information provided upon signing up, so nobody felt like they were repeating themselves or wasting a session by going over basic info again. Plus, Teladoc sessions lasted a full hour, which is almost unheard of in the online therapy space and helped us really delve deep during our appointments. No other company on this list offers hour-long therapy sessions, and most online therapy sessions last between 30 and 45 minutes.
Across the board, our therapists were professional and incredibly informed but also relatable and kind. I especially liked the fact that my testers’ therapists respected their lifestyles and the specific challenges they were facing—in one case, the therapist of a busy mom of four was careful not to over-assign homework that my tester clearly stated she would not be able to complete, while another of my testers welcomed homework and felt like it really helped her. In this way, I can see just how tailored Teladoc’s services are to each individual client, which is how therapy should be.
I also surveyed 100 Teladoc users and found that their experiences mirrored those of my testers. When it came to rating the company as either very good or excellent, Teladoc scored in the top three of all 55 reviewed online therapy companies. Also, 75% of users said that their Teladoc therapist met all of their needs—this was the highest score out of every single company by far.
What Users Say
“Overall, I was very satisfied with the quality of care I received at Teladoc. Having previously only participated in face-to-face therapy, I was a little nervous at the idea of trying it online. While the therapists I tried were different from one another in their individual styles and approaches, they both felt qualified, warm, caring, and attentive to my needs. They were available at times that suited my busy schedule, responded to my messages quickly, and worked to accommodate my needs.”
- Teladoc tester new to online therapy
Things to Consider
In my opinion, there’s not much to complain about when it comes to Teladoc. However, while the company does accept a wide range of insurance plans, my testers paid upwards of $90 even with insurance, and they didn’t find out what they would owe per session until they’d already signed up for services. The out-of-pocket prices might deter some therapy seekers as well.

What I Like About Doctor on Demand
I find
Doctor on Demand
impressive in many ways. First of all, each of our three testers had a great experience with their providers, who were relatable and clearly skilled—no notes there. In addition, you can choose your own therapist; this, as I’ve pointed out, is unusual in online therapy. This made the Doctor on Demand process even more accessible and personalized to my testers, who all immediately found providers with whom they connected.
What stood out to me the most, though, was the company’s online psychiatry services. The teenage son of one of my adult therapy testers was looking for medication to treat his mounting anxiety, and my tester, having had a great experience with her Doctor on Demand therapist, suggested a Doctor on Demand psychiatrist.
Not only does this company offer therapy services for kids
and
teens (another unusual standout in telehealth, where companies like Talkspace only treat ages 13 and up) but kids and teens also have access to psychiatry services—which is not something you’ll find with other online therapy services. And not only that, my teen tester had a great experience with his psychiatrist.
The ability to choose his own psychiatrist made my teen tester more comfortable and secure, and even though that psychiatrist informed my tester and his mom that he was not specifically a child psychiatrist per se, it was immediately apparent that this was the right provider for my teen. He and his mom appreciated both his psychiatrist’s candor and the detailed and personal process of his treatment.
This psychiatrist spoke to my teen’s mom alone, the teen alone, and both of them together, and asked questions they’d never been asked before during a first appointment; namely, the psychiatrist wanted a detailed family history, and asked which medications my teen’s mom had tried in the past, because understanding what worked for this teen’s family would be a good place to start for him. This approach very much impressed me.
The 100 users of Doctor on Demand I surveyed had similarly positive experiences: 88% rated Doctor on Demand as very good or excellent overall, and 91% felt the same about their providers’ qualifications.
What Users Say
“We especially appreciated the time our psychiatrist took to explain the functions and side effects of the medication he was going to prescribe our teen; we felt prepared for the future and the possible experiences that might accompany our son’s treatment. And in each of our follow-up sessions, our psychiatrist specifically asked about side effects as well as our teen’s mood and behavior.”
- Doctor on Demand tester whose teen son tried its psychiatry services
Things to Consider
I have a few minor bones to pick with Doctor on Demand. You have to create an account before you can see any provider bios, and one of my testers lamented that she was not able to search for a therapist who was queer-affirming or a BIPOC ally. There’s also no way to contact your provider between sessions, which I think would enhance the Doctor on Demand experience even more.

What I Like About TherapyDen
TherapyDen
is actually an online therapy directory rather than an online therapy company like the other companies on this list—it’s basically an online phone book for therapists. What stands out to me, though, is how incredibly (and unusually) inclusive this directory is. I had two testers try out TherapyDen, one in Kansas and one in New York, and both were impressed with the sheer number of search filters available—15, to be exact. This is what stood out to me as well.
If you are looking for a therapist who is part of the LGBTQ community, there’s a filter for that. If you are looking for a therapist of a specific ethnicity, there’s a filter for that. If you’re looking for a therapist who speaks a certain language, is familiar with a certain faith, is a certain gender, guess what—filters all around. Plus, TherapyDen’s “specialty” filter includes options like “Cultural and Systemic Oppression,” “Gender Identity,” “Historical/Intergenerational Trauma,” and “LGBTQ Issues.” Literally nowhere else have I found this level of inclusion.
And not only that, but the therapists we found on TherapyDen? Excellent professionals in their own right.
In addition to having testers look for their own therapists on TherapyDen, I also personally evaluated this directory—along with 26 other
online therapy directories
—across 37 different hypothetical scenarios in which someone might be looking for a therapist. These scenarios included everything from searching for services for treatment-resistant depression to couples counseling after an infidelity to finding treatment for a child diagnosed with autism, and I determined on a scale from 1 to 5 how many appropriate therapists I could find on each directory for each scenario.
Unsurprisingly, TherapyDen excelled in scenarios where inclusivity and diversity were important, such as finding a Black male or Black female therapist or finding a provider who could write a support letter for hormone therapy for a trans child.
In fact, TherapyDen recently added a callout on its homepage for the trans community, stating: “Let's promote self-affirmation, dignity, equal rights, and celebrate sexual and gender diversity. Meet these inspirational therapists who specialize in and are competently trained in supporting the trans community.” It then highlights nine bios of its providers who specialize in “therapy for the trans community.” This earns an A+ in my book.
What Users Say
“I was so impressed by how engaged and attuned my therapist was from the beginning. Their clinical expertise was apparent in how they asked intake questions and offered observations throughout our session.”
- TherapyDen tester looking for services in Kansas
Things to Consider
One thing to keep in mind when using TherapyDen is that the number of providers in your area may vary widely, especially if you use filters to narrow your search results. In Kansas, for example, the tester had to remove some of the filters to find any providers near them. So depending on what you are looking for, you might have to compromise a bit.

What I Like About E-Therapy Cafe
Six people—both individuals and couples—have tried out
E-Therapy Cafe
’s “boutique” online therapy services for me, and the results have been stellar. Basically, E-Therapy Cafe offers great therapy for low out-of-pocket prices. To me, this kind of accessibility is incredibly important; people who are uninsured or underinsured can turn to this service without having to sacrifice the quality of their therapy sessions.
My testers were generally thrilled with the services they received, both individual and couples (the latter of which doesn’t cost a penny more, unlike Talkspace and many other online therapy companies that also offer couples therapy). The therapists we worked with were clearly knowledgeable and were quickly able to develop a rapport, even when we expected otherwise—for example, when a 26-year old mother with a toddler and a baby on the way found that the older gentleman with whom she’d been paired was able to empathize with her and provide useful insights despite being in a very different phase of life. Across the board, our providers supported us with skill and compassion.
The E-Therapy Cafe team is small—only 13 clinicians—but because each provider is both a licensed clinical therapist and an expert-level life coach, any one of them is able to see clients anywhere in the country (while therapists are limited to practice only in the states in which they are licensed, coaches are not held to legal licensure restrictions, so most users would technically be seeking life coach services). This gave my testers the feel of a small private practice with the reach of a large telehealth service.
Plus, we found that scheduling was extremely flexible, with sessions available late at night and on weekends, which made E-Therapy Cafe even more accessible.
In my survey of 100 current and past users of E-Therapy Cafe, 90% said they were still using this service today—a high number compared to the rest of the 55 online therapy companies I researched and reviewed. In addition, these users especially appreciated E-Therapy Cafe’s early morning, evening, and weekend availability, with 43% (more than at most other companies) saying all of their scheduling needs were met in this way.
What Users Say
“​​[My therapist] was funny and a little quirky, but I think our dynamic worked. He was kind and attentive. From the way he responded and what I read of him in his impressive bio, he seemed very qualified to talk me through some of the things I brought up. He was able to validate my feelings and encourage me as I worked through them and in the end, I was very happy with who the company chose for me.”
- E-Therapy Cafe tester, a busy mom
Things to Consider
My main issue with E-Therapy Cafe is the dearth of therapists licensed in certain states. Although technically these clinicians can provide “coaching” services anywhere, it would be nice to be able to count on therapy services too (though none of my testers had any complaints about the coaching they received). I’d also love to see more diversity amongst the E-Therapy Cafe providers.
Other Options
Regain

How I Review Online Therapy Services
I’ve spent years testing and surveying real therapy users and reviewing their experiences to help rate and share the best online therapy services. You can see a full breakdown of our online therapy
research and testing process
on Verywell Mind. Here is an overview of how I capture and evaluate firsthand experience and survey data.
Firsthand Experience
I had 65 different people sign up for talk therapy with all 55 online therapy companies reviewed. Over the past three years, I’ve published 153 reviews (here are our reviews for
BetterHelp
and
Talkspace
, for instance). I had my testers try out each company's therapy services, and if the company offered other services (like couples therapy and kid or teen therapy) we tested those too.
I asked each tester to try each service for at least a month, which usually meant between two and six sessions at each company. I also asked testers to switch therapists partway through, if possible, in order to get a holistic idea of the offerings and quality of each company. My testers were located all across the United States and abroad, meaning I gained extensive information about each company's services in a variety of areas.
User Surveys
I surveyed more than 8,000 therapy clients who are currently using one of the 55 online therapy companies tested or who had used one in the recent past, as well as users of 27 different online therapy directories. I asked users to evaluate each company's services, from their therapist's qualifications to whether they found a therapist who met all their needs to how they'd rate the company overall—all told, I gathered 350 different data points.
Paying for Online Therapy
Some online therapy companies offer subscriptions, where you pay upfront for a month of services, while others charge per session. These out-of-pocket prices can be fixed, or they can be based on your location or your provider’s credentials.
In addition, some companies are in-network with insurance plans. Generally, the company’s website will either list the plans it accepts or allow you to search for your plan during or after sign-up. If your plan isn’t accepted, sometimes an online therapy company will offer a superbill for you to submit to your insurance company for reimbursement.
Medication and Online Therapy
Therapists, whether online or in-person, are not allowed to prescribe medication. (In a handful of states
psychologists can
, but this is the exception.) For the most part, only
psychiatrists
and psychiatric nurse practitioners can prescribe medication to treat mental illness. If an online therapy company also offers psychiatry or medication management services, that means that they have prescribers on staff, separate from their therapists.
Psychiatry services are generally more expensive than therapy services, and the follow-up sessions are shorter than therapy sessions (usually around 15 minutes).
When looking for online psychiatry, make sure the company in question offers this service—they will advertise it as a separate service from online therapy. While signing up for the company, you will have to indicate that you are looking for psychiatry or medication management. From there, the company will usually match you with a prescriber.
