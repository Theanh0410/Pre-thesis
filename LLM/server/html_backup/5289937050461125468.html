Methodology

Sleep Foundation

Product Research Methodology: Our Process

At SleepFoundation.org, we personally test every sheet set featured in our product reviews and guides. Our hands-on approach allows us to evaluate sheets using different performance factors, and provide our readers with accurate, data-based recommendations.

Guidelines we follow for testing sheets include:

  1. To ensure objective findings, we test all sheet sets using different mattress types. Testing the sheets on foam, latex, and hybrid mattresses allows us to determine baseline performance ratings for breathability, softness, and overall comfort.
  2. Some of our performance criteria are based on observable characteristics, such as fit and ease of cleaning. These ratings do not require hands-on tests, but still factor into our reviews and recommendations.
  3. We conduct new tests throughout the year and revisit our current picks after each round of testing has concluded. If need be, we’ll update our product guides to reflect our most recent findings.

There are two camps of sleepers: Those who stay cool in bed despite the weather and multiple layers of bedding, and those for whom zero sheets are simply too hot. The latter group tends to spend the night kicking off any coverings, lest they burst into flames, and if you’re in this camp, you know this is only a slight exaggeration. 

But what you might not know is that there are bed sheets specifically designed to help take your temperature down and alleviate your bedtime misery. With so many sets available, plus lots of jargon and marketing claims to decipher, our testing team of hot sleepers did the research to help you find the best cooling sheets out there. We’ll jump right into our Test Lab’s top picks for the best cooling sheets below, then we’ll talk about how they actually work and how to find the right set for your bed.

How We Test: Rigor, Data, and Sleeper Feedback

Our testing team understands that every sleeper has different likes and dislikes when it comes to sheets and other sleep products. We’ve created an objective rating system that takes individual considerations into account, allowing us to narrow down the pros and cons of different products and determine who benefits most from using them.

We know bedding plays a role in how well you sleep — the right sheet set can be a game-changer, while sheets that don’t align with your preferences can lead to restless nights of tossing and turning. In our Seattle-based Test Lab, we evaluate each sheet set with the same four-step process. Each component of this system is outlined below.

Construction analysis: Our tests begin with a thorough examination of how the sheets and pillowcases are constructed. We make note of material, weave, thread count, and other specifications that help us determine qualities like longevity and breathability.

Product testing: In order to thoroughly test bedding, each member of our team has the opportunity to lie on a mattress with all four components of a sheet set. We’ll change positions to see if the fitted sheet remains intact, use heat guns to gauge how well each item regulates temperature, and assign ratings for qualities like cooling, warmth, and overall comfort.

Field testing: Field testers play a pivotal role in our rating process. They’ll sleep with a sheet set in their own home for weeks at a time, making daily notes that help us understand how well the bedding performs in the long term.

User testing: Experiences from verified sheet owners are also important. We’ll sift through surveys and product reviews to collect feedback from consumers — this helps us fine-tune our ratings to ensure they reflect real-world experiences.

What Are Cooling Sheets?

Cooling sheets are bed sheets that are made out of materials and weaves that are breathable and moisture-wicking to allow air to move around your skin so your body’s natural cooling mechanisms can do their thing. They’re made to feel light and soft against your skin, rather than scratchy, clingy, or suffocating.

Cooling sheets are best if you tend to sleep hot, but they’re also good if:

  • It’s summer or you live in a hot climate
  • You experience hot flashes or night sweats
  • You share the bed with a cold sleeper who’s comfortable with a mountain of blankets on their side

Choosing the Best Cooling Sheets

Cooling sheets are designed to resist heat buildup and allow you to sleep comfortably without overheating. “You can have a breathable mattress with all the cooling technology in the world, but it won’t help much if you’re sleeping in heat-trapping sheets,” says Daniel Noyed, Senior Product Editor. 

If you’re a hot sleeper, sheets made of breathable materials like cotton, linen, and bamboo-derived fabrics are going to be your best friends, adds Noyed. “As a hot sleeper myself, once I made the switch to linen sheets, I never looked back.”

Cooling sheets pull off the miraculous feat of keeping you cool in two major ways:

  1. They’re made with breathable materials that allow plenty of airflow to help heat escape.
  2. They’re good at wicking away moisture so it can evaporate and keep you from waking up in a pool of sweat.

That’s why when choosing sheets for their cool factor, material and weave will be more important than thread count and aesthetics. Though a higher thread count (the total number of threads in a square inch) can equate to more durability and softness, it’s not as important for people looking for cooling sheets.

Be a Material Girl (or Guy)

Choose breathable, natural fibers, says Sarah Silverman, Psy.D., a psychologist and holistic sleep wellness consultant. “Regulating your core body temperature during the night can make a huge difference in your overall sleep quality, so breathable materials are optimal to promote cooler temperatures.” 

Her recommendations: Hypoallergenic materials like cotton, linen, silk, or bamboo.  “Synthetic fibers tend to be less breathable and trap more heat at night. This goes for bedding/sheets as well as your pajamas or sleepwear.”

Here’s a quick cheat sheet:

Weave Your Way Into Cool Nights

Weave refers to how the threads are layered to create sheets. Generally speaking, a percale weave allows greater breathability. Percale sheets have a crisp feel and a matte-like appearance, sort of like your classic dress shirt. 

In contrast, sateen sheets are softer, smoother, and shinier. These sheets tend to feel heavier and trap more heat, so we generally recommend percale sheets for better cooling.

Other Factors to Consider

Color and Design

Aesthetics may not feel as important to you as not feeling like you’re on a rotisserie all night, but you still want your bedroom to look great, right? When comparing sheet sets, finding ones that work for your color and style scheme will make it more likely that you’ll use them regularly. If texture is important to you, keep in mind that silk sheets or sheets with a sateen weave tend to be shinier and have a more pronounced drape, while linen sheets and sheets with a percale weave usually have a crisper finish.

Quality and Cost

Cooling sheets can range in price from around $50 to as much as $500. The reason for this wide range is the diversity of materials and styles found in cooling sheets. High-end materials like Egyptian cotton and silk tend to cost more, and prices can also be higher for organic or specialty materials.

It is true that you’ll usually have better, longer-lasting results when you buy high-quality versions of your preferred cooling fabric. High-end materials can stand the test of time and keep performing well despite being used and washed many times. But if you’re on a budget, there are many affordable cooling sheet options as well.

Ease of Care

Some sheets require special handling to avoid damage when washing and drying them. Cotton, bamboo viscose, and Tencel sheets tend to be the easiest cooling sheets to clean. Silk is the most challenging to care for. For hot sleepers who may have to wash your sheets more often, it helps to have sheets that can be quickly and conveniently machine-washed and dried.

Other Ways to Keep Cool While You’re Sleeping

Cooling sheets can have a big impact on your sleep quality, but they’ll be more effective when used in tandem with other cooling products. Consider pairing your fabulous cooling sheets with these other products:

A cooling mattress: A cooling mattress and cooling bedding can reduce heat buildup when you’re trying to sleep. Certain types of mattresses, including latex and hybrid mattresses, are better at keeping you cool. Special cooling features, including gel-infused layers, are engineered for better temperature regulation. 

Cooling bedding: Sometimes a cooling mattress topper can prevent sleeping hot without investing in an entirely new mattress. You can also look for cooling pillows, which reduce heat buildup around your head and neck. A cooling comforter can provide light warmth and is often a better choice than a heavy blanket that holds in heat. Cooling pillows and bedding use breathable materials to prevent heat retention. 

Breathable pajamas: Choosing pajamas with less skin coverage, including shorts and short-sleeve tops, can help avoid trapping heat against your body. Go with materials like silk, lightweight cotton, and linen to keep cool.
